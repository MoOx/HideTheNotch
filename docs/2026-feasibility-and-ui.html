Feasibility study, August 2026

Seven families, one interface

What families 01, 03, 07, 08, 09, 11 and 12 actually require, scored by complexity, with the parameters proposed for each. Then the interface, reduced to a single screen and five gestures.

Follow-up to "Hiding the notch, nine years on" 7 families kept out of 13
01

The cost is in the foundations, not the families

The study's first result, and it changes how to plan: taken on their own, five of the seven families are one to three days of work. What is expensive is five shared pieces that have to be written once. Once they exist, adding a family becomes an evening's work, and that is what makes a living catalogue possible.

Foundation A

Geometry

Device table plus runtime measurement of the safe area and model. Exposes a cutout box {x, y, w, h, shape} in points and in pixels.

Required by all 7, about 3 d

Foundation B

Recipe and engine

JSON describes the wallpaper; one engine renders it on screen and offscreen at native pixels. Preview and export parity is the one non negotiable thing.

Required by all 7, about 4 d

Foundation C

SkSL layer

Three reusable shaders: dithering at plus or minus 1 LSB, fractal noise (fbm), and gradient interpolation in linear light.

03, 08, 09, 12, about 3 d

Foundation D

Procedural rig

Seeded pseudo random generator plus a coverage guarantee: a black base laid under the decor, whose shape derives from the cutout.

07, 08, 11, 12, about 2 d

Foundation E

Image analysis

Downsample to 96 x 208, readPixels(), summed area table, framing solver. A few milliseconds.

09 only, about 3 d

Foundation E is the only single use piece, and also the riskiest. That is the clearest argument for keeping family 09 out of the first pass.

02

The seven families, scored

Four axes out of 5: rendering (drawing difficulty), parameters (how many settings to expose and how subtle they are), content (art direction needed up front), and risk (what may not work). Estimates assume foundations A and B are already in place.

03

Build order

The order below is not a ranking by importance: it is a dependency chain. Each step delivers a piece the next one needs, and each step produces an app that works.

1 01, solid bar: proves foundations A and B end to end, on the family where any alignment error shows immediately. +0.5 d
2 11, decaying stripes: almost free once 01 is done, the same drawing repeated. Doubles the catalogue for next to nothing. +1 d
3 03, dithered fade: forces foundation C to be written. Banding is settled here, once and for all. +2 d and foundation C
4 12, pure generative: reuses foundation C, adds foundation D. Delivers a first launch with no photo and no permission. +3 d and foundation D
5 08, organic decor: the procedural rig already exists, only the art direction of the patterns remains. +3 d
6 07, hanging object: same rig as 08, plus a drawing constraint. Can be added object by object, after launch. +2 d then 0.5 d per object
7 09, content camouflage: foundation E, single use. Treat it as a separate project, with a way out. +5 d and foundation E

Natural cut after step 4: foundations A to D, four families (bar, stripes, fade, generative), all robust, none depending on graphic work. That is a complete and honest app. Steps 5 to 7 are what make it remarkable, and they can come later.

04

The interface: one screen, five gestures

The app has a single object, the wallpaper being edited, and a single output, an image. Any added navigation would be decoration. Hence the proposal: no tabs, no menu, no account, one screen. The wallpaper takes 100 percent of the surface, the controls float over it in glass, and most of the adjusting happens by touching the wallpaper directly.

The point that changes everything: the preview is not a mockup of a phone inside a phone. It is the wallpaper, full screen, under the device's real cutout. You judge the result by looking at it, not by imagining it.

The gesture vocabulary

swipe left, rightchange effect family, like a camera filter
pinchreframe and reposition the photo
drag a handleadjust the effect directly on the wallpaper
long presshide the whole interface to judge
shakecontact support
05

One rule: three controls at most

Each family exposes at most three controls, all live, all reversible, never showing a numeric value except where the number means something. Whatever does not fit in three controls does not fit in the app.

Hard constraints are never error messages. A handle reaching the bottom of the cutout does not stop silently: it butts, vibrates lightly, and shows a line. The user learns the physical rule by touching it, without being told.

The export format lives in the save sheet, not in a settings screen. It is the only moment where the question "for which phone?" really arises, and it is where you can generate a wallpaper for someone else's iPhone.

06

What I would cut

  • The bundled wallpaper library. v1 had one; it is heavy, it dates, and it raises rights questions. Family 12 replaces it with an endless generator that weighs nothing.
  • Any settings screen. The export format goes in the save sheet, calibration triggers after the first export, nothing is left to configure.
  • The help screen. Replaced by haptic stops and a single sentence in the export sheet, at the moment it is useful.
  • Family 09 in v1. It is the finest promise and the only one that can fail on the user's photo. It deserves to arrive on its own, when it is genuinely good.
