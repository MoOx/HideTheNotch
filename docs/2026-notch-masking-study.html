Research note, August 2026

Hiding the notch, nine years on

A post mortem of HideTheNotch (2017), an inventory of the masking techniques that exist today, and thirteen families of solutions to settle before writing a single line of code.

Repo · MoOx/HideTheNotch v1 · RN 0.49 · nov. 2017 Cible · Expo SDK 56 / RN 0.85
01

What v1 did

The 2017 app is 789 lines. A full screen ImageBackground, a stack of PNGs laid over it at position: absolute / top: 0, and captureRef() from react-native-view-shot to photograph that view and push it into the camera roll. Four masks: Rounded Notch, Rounded Slim Notch, Hard Notch, Hard Slim Notch, four bitmaps with no parameters.

It was the right answer at the time: exactly one device in the world had a notch, and it was entirely described by src/platform.js in eight lines, isIPhoneX = height === 812 && width === 375.

What aged well

  • The core intuition: on OLED, #000000 placed next to a cutout literally makes it vanish.
  • The product gesture: you do not install a "theme", you export an image that the user applies.
  • The catalogue of bundled backgrounds plus personal photo import, with author credit shown.

The five ceilings

  • One known device. Every new iPhone shifted the mask out of alignment.
  • Bitmap masks. Nothing to adjust: no height, no radius, no colour.
  • Export equals screenshot. The source photo is shrunk to screen size before capture. No reframing, no zoom (the ScrollView pinch is commented out in the code).
  • No Dynamic Island, it did not exist.
  • The last mile untreated: nothing stops iOS from re-zooming the wallpaper when it is applied, which breaks pixel alignment.
02

Pourquoi l'astuce marche (et quand elle casse)

Every iPhone with a cutout is OLED: a black pixel is an off pixel, optically identical to the panel around the camera. That is the only reason masking works, and it imposes three non negotiable rules.

1. Absolute black, not "almost black". #010101 shows on OLED in a dark room.

2. PNG only. JPEG produces block artefacts around a black / image boundary: the cutout becomes visible again.

3. Dithered gradients. A fade to black in 8 bits bands sharply on OLED. Noise of plus or minus 1 LSB has to be injected.

03

Thirteen masking families

Each family is a generator, not an image: a function that takes the cutout geometry and returns a stack of layers. Switch device below and the thirteen previews recompute. That is exactly the model the app should implement.

04

The pipeline this needs

Seven of these thirteen families are impossible on the 2017 architecture: they need dithered gradients, blend modes, procedural noise and an export above screen resolution. The structural change is simple to state: stop photographing a React Native view.

Model A recipe, not an image One JSON object describes everything: source (photo, gradient, procedural), transform (framing, zoom, rotation), stack of parameterised masks, target device geometry. Serialisable, therefore shareable, versionable and regenerable for another phone.
Preview Skia canvas at screen scale @shopify/react-native-skia draws the recipe at 120 fps, with pinch and pan on the photo through Reanimated 4 and Gesture Handler. Gradients, fractal noise and custom shaders are native.
Export Offscreen surface at native pixels Skia.Surface.MakeOffscreen(1290, 2796) replays the same recipe at the device's real resolution, then makeImageSnapshot(), encodeToBytes(PNG), expo-media-library. Quality 1:1, independent of the preview screen.
Shell Expo SDK 56 or later, New Architecture expo-glass-effect gives the real Liquid Glass toolbar (native UIVisualEffectView) on iOS 26 and above, with an expo-blur fallback below. @expo/ui for SwiftUI sheets and pickers, expo-haptics for snapping feedback.
Android Simpler than iOS, in fact Same Skia engine, same recipes. And above all WallpaperManager.setBitmap() applies the wallpaper directly, with no gallery and no cropping, so the main iOS obstacle does not exist. does not exist.
05

The real problem: applying the wallpaper

A pixel perfect mask is worthless if iOS shifts it by four percent while applying it. That is where v1 stopped, its help text saying "set it to Still and align it at the bottom", and it is where the difference between a toy and a tool is decided.

What breaks alignment

  • Perspective zoom, iOS enlarges the wallpaper by about 4 % for gyroscope parallax.
  • Spatial scenes (iOS 26), subject separated from background and made to move: misalignment guaranteed.
  • Depth effect on the lock screen.
  • The crop editor that opens on every "Choose a photo" and invites pinching.

The countermeasures

  • Export at the exact native resolution, so iOS shows the image 1:1 by default, with no cropping.
  • An App Intent plus a supplied Shortcut: the Set Wallpaper action applies the image without opening the editor. One gesture. To be validated in real conditions, but it is the project's strongest differentiator.
  • A calibration target: a test wallpaper letting the user measure and correct a residual offset, then remembered for every export.
  • Instructions targeted per iOS version, not a generic blurb.
06

Is it worth it?

The calendar is on your side. The iPhone 18 Pro is expected in September 2026 with a much smaller cutout and, according to leaks, offset to the left. A centred black bar becomes absurd on an asymmetric cutout: the whole "wallpaper that hides the hole" category becomes an open design problem again, exactly when a well made app can find its place.

The competition is weak but entrenched. The App Store is full of Dynamic Island wallpaper galleries: subscriptions, ads, 90 percent of the content behind a paywall, no notion of real geometry. None of them offers a generator: parameterised recipe, native resolution export, your own photo. That is the opening, and it matches what HideTheNotch already was in 2017.

The question to settle first. Not "which framework", but how many families at launch. Three done well (parametric bar, dithered fade, generative) beat thirteen done roughly, and families 05 to 09, the ones that need drawing, are what will get the app talked about.
